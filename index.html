Game Sheets

Our NFL anytime-touchdown picks, tracked in the open. Every pick is logged before kickoff, bet with imaginary money, and graded after the game. Wins and losses both stay on the page.

How it works

  1. The model rates every player. For each game it estimates how many points each team will score, then splits that among the players on the field by how they have been used: carries, targets, red-zone touches. The result is each player's chance to score a touchdown.
  2. We compare it to the sportsbook. A price like +300 means the book thinks the player scores about 25% of the time. If our chance is higher than the price implies, the model sees an edge. Often it is wrong, which is why we track it.
  3. We pick before kickoff. About an hour before the game we take the current DraftKings and FanDuel prices and the official inactive list, then log the picks. The last log before kickoff is the one that counts. Nothing is changed afterwards.
  4. We grade it with paper money. Each pick is an imaginary $10 at the best price available when we logged it. If a player is inactive, or hurt in the game and the books void his bet, the pick is void and costs nothing.

The picks, every game

FavoriteThe player the model says is most likely to score.
Non-lead-backSame, but skipping each team's lead running back, who usually carries the shortest price.
Margin pickAmong players the model rates between 25% and 40%, the one whose chance beats the sportsbook's price by the most. The cooldown version skips anyone who was this pick in the last two weeks.
Receiving yardsA long-odds alternate yardage line (for example 70+ yards at +200) where the model's chance beats the price by the most.

v3 and v4 are two versions of the model run side by side (v4 also looks at how the opponent defends each position). "Book fav" is the sportsbook favorite, just the shortest price on the board, as a yardstick: if the model cannot beat that, it adds nothing.

What we are watching

  • In backtests on earlier 2026 games the margin pick did not show a profit: where the model disagrees most with the book, the model is often the one that is wrong. The cooldown version exists to stop it from picking the same player every week.
  • The non-lead-back pick has been the weakest so far.
  • The receiving-yards pick is new in Week 12, so there is almost nothing to judge it on yet.
  • New weeks are added to this page as they are played, and the model only changes in the open, with the change written down first.
